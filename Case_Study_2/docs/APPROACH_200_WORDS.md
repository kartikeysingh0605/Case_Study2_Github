# Case Study 2 — Approach (200-word limit)

I implemented an offline traffic-intersection analytics pipeline for a fixed daytime camera using Python, OpenCV, a pretrained YOLO11n detector, and ByteTrack. The original 10-minute-56-second recording contains 19,699 frames at 30 FPS; I processed every frame to preserve short crossings and stationary-to-moving transitions. I manually defined polygons for four approach arms, a junction region, queue regions, and directional stop lines. For each persistent track, the pipeline records a detected entry arm and confirms an exit arm after the vehicle leaves the junction. These events populate an entry-count summary and a 4-by-4 turning-movement table. Within approach queue regions, track displacement over time estimates whether a vehicle is stopped; maximum simultaneous stopped-vehicle counts provide queue estimates. For tracks with observable queue-start and stop-line-crossing events, the system measures waiting time. It exports an annotated video, per-track CSV, summary CSVs, and JSON. The pipeline is reproducible, but these outputs are preliminary: distant and occluded vehicles can fragment into multiple IDs, different classes can overlap on one truck, and manually placed zones and pixel-based stopping thresholds can bias counts and waits. I therefore report unresolved trajectories separately and do not present unverified totals as ground truth.
